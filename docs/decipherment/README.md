# Rongorongo decipherment: what held, and what failed

This note is a plain-English account of an independent, non-profit computational study of rongorongo, the undeciphered script of Rapa Nui (Easter Island), covering rounds 1 through 5. The sign numbers are Thomas Barthel’s 1958 catalog, taken from the transcription prepared by the Cercle d’Études sur l’Île de Pâques et la Polynésie (CEIPP) and published on [rongorongo.org](http://kohaumotu.org/rongorongo_org/copy.html), with a working mirror at kohaumotu.org. CEIPP’s own notice allows those materials to be copied when the source is acknowledged and the distribution is non-profit. The structural patterns below survive the tests that were fixed before the scores were read. Every attempt to turn a sign into a sound or a word failed on text that had been held out of the choice.

A Barthel code is a three-digit sign number. A ligature is two or more signs written as one group; the transcription joins them with a dot or a colon. In the counts below, a sign is that number after a ligature has been split and after the small letters Barthel used for variants have been removed. The detailed notes call that unit a stem. A shuffle null keeps the same signs and randomly reorders them, then asks how often the pattern still appears. When none of the random draws matches the real text, the pattern is rarer than that rearrangement produces. A held-out check is a second look at tablets or lines that were not used to choose the claim.

## Findings that held up

These are facts about sign order and copying. None of them is a translation.

**Mamari lunar calendar.** On tablet C (Mamari), lines Ca6–Ca9, the crescent coded `040` occurs 28 times. Sign `143` occurs once, immediately before sign `152`, and that pair occurs nowhere else in tablets A–V. Guy (1990) and Horley (2011) read `152` as the full moon and `143` as the night before it. Counting `040` from `152` back to the first delimiter and forward to the last gives 13 crescents before `152` and 13 after it. Guy’s own count was 12 before and 13 after; the extra crescent in this transcription was left in place. The same passage has a run of six `040` signs before `152` and a run of five after it, which is the shape of the unnamed *kokore* night series in the recorded lists. Shuffling the 101 signs of the passage (5,000 draws, seed 0) produced that pair of runs 0 times. The Monte Carlo bound is 1/5001. The calendar is 101 signs out of 14,841 in A–V and holds 28 of the 152 signs coded `040`. Detail: [track1_mamari_calendar.md](track1_mamari_calendar.md). The same 28, the same 13/13 split, and the same delimiter gaps were still there when the CEIPP drawings were rechecked: [round5b_ceipp_recheck.md](round5b_ceipp_recheck.md).

**Genealogy handoff on Small Santiago.** On line Gv6, four phrases have the shape `200 X Y.076`, and three of them hand the second name to the next phrase: 280, then 730, then 517. Shuffling the 28 groups of that line produced 3 or more handoffs in 0 of 5,000 draws. Shuffling the whole verso, keeping line lengths, produced 3 handoffs in 0 of 2,000 draws. The same formula does not hand off anywhere else that was searched. The English sentence “X begat Y” remains a published hypothesis (Butinov and Knorozov 1956, as discussed by Davletshin 2012). The handoff is the result. Detail: [track3_genealogy_staff.md](track3_genealogy_staff.md).

**Staff sections marked by a bar.** On the Santiago Staff (text I), the vertical stroke is coded `999` (97 codes, of which 96 are a bar by itself). The next group contains sign `076` after 91 of those 96 bars. A shuffle that leaves the bars in place and reorders the other groups on each line reaches 91 or more in 0 of 2,000 draws. Detail: [track3_genealogy_staff.md](track3_genealogy_staff.md).

**Sign `076` ends its group, and on the Staff the gaps pile up at 3 and 4 signs.** Outside the anchor passages, a group that contains exactly one `076` and at least one other sign ends on `076` in 393 of 408 groups on the Staff and in 97 of 105 groups on the other tablets. Putting that one `076` in a random slot of its own group reaches those counts in 0 of 500 draws, on each cut. Detail: [round2_trackB_anchor_crib.md](round2_trackB_anchor_crib.md). Separately, the Staff has 564 signs coded `076`. The 550 gaps between successive `076` signs on the same line include 197 gaps of length 3 and 137 of length 4. A shuffle of each line’s signs reaches 197 gaps of length 3 in 0 of 1,000 draws. That spacing is a fact about the Staff. It is not, by itself, the creation chant discussed under the failures below. Detail: [track4_native_readings.md](track4_native_readings.md).

**Parallel passages and repeated substitutions.** A search that keeps a repeated passage only when the longer copy covers at least 8 signs and at least 80 percent of the aligned columns match found 99 passages. Shuffling signs inside each side (24 draws, seed 0) produced a maximum score of 0, so all 99 sit above that null. The long shared text is the Great Tradition on tablets H (Great Santiago), P (Great St. Petersburg), and Q (Small St. Petersburg). Eight substitution pairs inside those passages clear a pre-set gate (at least 3 occurrences, and a per-pair null fraction of at most 1/100, from 400 re-pairings of the mismatch columns). The busiest is `400` with `600` (9 times; null fraction 1/401). The other seven are `002`–`021` (7), `001`–`011` (6), `254`–`256` (6), `056`–`084` (5), `008`–`081` (4), `280`–`290` (4), and `381`–`385` (3). Detail: [round2_trackC_parallels.md](round2_trackC_parallels.md).

**Bird signs in the 400s and 600s swap as a class.** Guy (2006) classes Barthel series 400–409 with series 600–699 as birds. In the 99 passages, 18 of 34 cross-hundred substitutions share a pictured class, and all 18 are that bird class (including `400` opposite `600` nine times). A shuffle of the class labels reaches that count in 23 of 500 draws (fraction 0.046). Detail: [round4a_pictograph_rebus.md](round4a_pictograph_rebus.md). The count of 18 was unchanged in the CEIPP recheck. Placed later in the Round 5 family of 24 tests and corrected with them (Holm 1979), the adjusted value is 0.7186, above the 0.05 line used there. The count of 18 was left as recorded. Detail: [round5a_heldout_pictograph.md](round5a_heldout_pictograph.md) and [round5b_ceipp_recheck.md](round5b_ceipp_recheck.md).

**H and Q are the closest copies.** On the shared passages, the share of aligned columns that differ is 0.177903 for H–Q (534 columns), 0.184282 for P–Q, and 0.189907 for H–P. Resampling the 99 passages 1,000 times and rebuilding the tree places H and Q as the closest pair in 66.3 percent of replicates. Where all three tablets have a sign, the same pair shares the most sign numbers against the third in 67.9 percent of a column resample. Three witnesses do not name a scribe. Detail: [round4_trackD_dating_stemma.md](round4_trackD_dating_stemma.md).

**Radiocarbon dates the wood, and only for some objects.** Tablet D (Échancrée) is the early outlier: 474±13 BP, and 1493–1509 cal AD at 68.3 percent after a felling offset of 50±5 years (1483–1520 at 95.4 percent). Ferrara and colleagues warn that the age is a limit on when the tree died, and that foreign wood can be driftwood or ship timber. D shares no significant passage with H, P, or Q, so that date does not date the Great Tradition. Tablet Q (Small St. Petersburg) is the date Orliac measured as 80±40 BP. The 95 percent ranges quoted by Wieczorek, Frankiewicz, Oskolski, and Horley (2021) are 1680–1740, 1800–1930, and 1950–1960. Ferrara et al. (2024), citing Horley (2021), call 1812–1836 the most reliable slice of a SHCal20 recalibration. Sources: Ferrara, Tassoni, Kromer, Wacker, Friedrich, Tonini, Lastilla, Ravanelli, and Talamo (2024), *Scientific Reports* 14:2794, [doi:10.1038/s41598-024-53063-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53063-7); Orliac (2005), *Archaeology in Oceania* 40:115–119; Wieczorek et al. (2021), *Journal of Island and Coastal Archaeology*, [doi:10.1080/15564894.2021.1950874](https://doi.org/10.1080/15564894.2021.1950874). The repository’s table and the citations it copied are in [round4_trackD_dating_stemma.md](round4_trackD_dating_stemma.md).

## What failed

These attempts were specified, scored, and not adopted. A miss on a corpus-wide sound list does not by itself overturn a scholar’s reading of one passage. It means the values, applied as stated, do not spell the attested words above the comparisons that were fixed in advance.

**Sound cribs from the anchors.** Four maps gave the Mamari crescent, sign `200`, and sign `076` the first syllable of a cited Rapanui word (night, moon, man, child, and the patronymic *ure*). Outside the anchor passages, strings of two to four of these signs spelled a target word (a night name, *marama*, *mahina*, *poki*, *tama*, *ure*, or *tangata*) zero times. Fifteen of 500 draws of random attested syllables did spell at least one target word. A second pass on a larger old-Rapanui sample (Thomson’s chants, Metoro’s recitations, and Routledge’s short formula) still has zero target hits. Detail: [round2_trackB_anchor_crib.md](round2_trackB_anchor_crib.md) and [round3_trackC_rapanui_corpus.md](round3_trackC_rapanui_corpus.md).

**A Rapanui syllabary.** The sign list has 630 types in 14,488 tokens. The Thomson chant sample, love song excluded, has 49 syllable types in 3,400 syllables. At 3,400 tokens the signs still have 394 types. A syllabary gate (about 45–70 types, and an inventory that flattens the way the syllables do) does not open. Published merges leave the list large: 613 types under the Pozdniakov and Pozdniakov (2007) hand list, 573 under Horley’s (2005) stated equivalences. No sign was paired with a syllable. Detail: [track2_syllabary_test.md](track2_syllabary_test.md) and [round2_trackA_variant_merge.md](round2_trackA_variant_merge.md).

**Candidate sign-strings against Rapanui word lengths.** Thomson’s words in that same sample have a mean length of 2.21 syllables (median 2). An unsupervised segmentation of the Great Tradition (tablets H, P, and Q), trained to prefer repeated strings and told where `076` ends a group, produces units of mean length 1.08 signs. Of 3,735 units, 3,597 are a single sign. The distance between that length histogram and the Thomson histogram is 0.6659 on a scale where 0 is the same histogram. A cut that uses only the boundary marks goes the other way (mean length 12.13 signs). Neither matches the word lengths. Detail: [round3_trackA_segmentation.md](round3_trackA_segmentation.md).

**Published sound values.** Fischer’s 1995 values for the Staff example (`600` *manu*, `606` *manu mau*, `700` *ika*, `008` *ra'a*, `076` as the copula phrase) score 26 phrase spans out of 181. The worse of the two pre-set comparisons, after a Holm correction across every scored proposal, is 0.750. Davletshin’s 2012 name values (`076` *ko*, `021` *'a*, `530` *ariki*) score 2 spans out of 54; the Holm-adjusted worse value is 1.000. His reading of crescent `040` as *tahi* ‘one’ scores 0 spans out of 41; the Holm-adjusted worse value is 1.000. A separate check of sign `200` as *tangata* used only tablets H through V and left out tablet G, including the Gv6 line that had suggested a “man” sign. It scores 16 phrase spans out of 20 valued spans. The shuffle value is 0.065 (2 of 31) and the random-sound value is 0.014 (7 of 500). The Holm-adjusted worse value is 0.258. None of these sets was adopted. Lists that were not available as sign-by-sign pairs (Davletshin 2022, Pozdniakov and Pozdniakov 2007, Horley, Guy 1990, Barthel 1958) were left empty rather than reconstructed. Detail: [../round4e_expert_values.md](../round4e_expert_values.md).

**Petroglyph matches.** Horley and Lee (2008) print Barthel numbers for rock-art motifs at Mata Ngarau (long-beaked birds, a hook or gourd, a rei miro, a sitting man, and others). Those picture matches were kept as iconography. A test of whether the matching signs cluster on particular tablets more tightly than ordinary common signs did not clear its bar: the eight most common signs that are not in the petroglyph set have a median shuffle value of 0.0005, and no petroglyph sign is both past the corrected threshold and past that median. The list of adopted word readings is empty. Detail: [round4_trackC_petroglyphs.md](round4_trackC_petroglyphs.md).

**Chant formulas.** The repeated sentences in Thomson’s 1891 chants are real. Ure Vaeiko’s Atua Matariri uses one copula frame in 41 of 48 verses, and the three name slots are usually 3 or 4 words wide (18 and 15 of 38 adjacent verse pairs). The Staff’s `076` gaps also peak at 3 and 4 signs. That resemblance was measured and was not adopted as a reading: tablet R, the object Thomson printed the chant with, has zero signs coded `076`, and Thomson already reported that the chant continued when a different photograph was substituted. Comparing recurring sign-strings on H, P, and Q with Rapanui formula shapes (burstiness, line position, and immediate repetition) produced no match under the pre-set bar, which required both a sign shuffle and a word shuffle to fall at or under 0.0036. The Zipf slopes (how steeply frequency falls with rank) differ by 0.3702, and the gate was 0.15. Detail: [track4_native_readings.md](track4_native_readings.md) and [round4_trackB_formula_match.md](round4_trackB_formula_match.md).

**The pictograph and rebus package, on 13 held-out tablets.** Round 4 assigned eight cited words (*marama*, *rakau*, *omotohi*, *tangata*, *manu*, *makohe*, *ika*, and *rima*) and asked whether those words, and whether signs of one pictured class, recur above a shuffle. Round 5A froze that list and scored it only on tablets that share no significant parallel with the tablets where the parallel and calendar counts were concentrated: D, F, I, J, L, M, N, O, S, T, U, V, and W (13 tablets; 88 lines and 4,088 signs after the copy rule). Mixed-word hits were 6 on 36 eligible windows. The sign shuffle was at least that high in 500 of 500 draws (add-one value 1.0000; Holm-adjusted 1.0000). Signs of one class sitting two apart were 65, against a shuffle mean of 59.096 (110 of 500; Holm-adjusted 1.0000). Bird-class substitutions could not be retested, because no significant passage has both tablets in the held-out set. The package does not survive. The plan was written down before these counts: [round5a_preregistration.md](round5a_preregistration.md). The scores: [round5a_heldout_pictograph.md](round5a_heldout_pictograph.md).

## Method

Each round stated the comparison before the scores were read. Round 5A went further and committed the plan as its own file before the held-out counts were computed. The usual null keeps each line’s signs, or each line’s words, and shuffles their order, so a pattern has to beat what the same inventory produces by chance. Claims that had already been looked at on the whole corpus were checked again on held-out tablets, and a Holm correction was applied so that a second look did not receive a fresh 5 percent gate. The analysis code can call a language-model provider. These tracks use only `MockProvider`, a stand-in that is never asked for a completion, and the recorded provider-call count is 0. No sign number was invented to improve a count, and no reading was written in where a cited list was missing.

## Round 5B: the CEIPP drawings, and why resolution is the limit

The headline counts do not move. Crescent totals, the 13/13 split, the delimiter gaps, the four Gv6 phrases and three handoffs, the Staff rate of 91 of 96, the H/Q span of 125 signs (107 matches, 16 mismatches), and the 18 bird substitutions are the same after the drawings were opened as they were in the vendored Barthel text. No Barthel number in the repository was replaced.

The CEIPP raster strips used for the check are about 74 pixels tall (Mamari strips 522 pixels wide, the others 622). At that size a crescent, a bar, and a full figure can be told apart. A catalog choice such as `044` versus `078` cannot. Vector pages on the same mirror trace each glyph and print a Barthel label beside it. Where that label and the vendored text disagree, the outline was not given a third number. The recheck logged 12 such disagreements, including Guy’s proposed renumbering of one stacked group, the `044`/`078` figure, an uncertain hand `006` where the text has `064`, and a label `048` where the text has `042`. All 12 have `adopted` set to false. The record is [round5b_ceipp_recheck.md](round5b_ceipp_recheck.md) and `data/decipherment/round5b_ceipp_recheck.json`.

That is why the next useful step is imagery that resolves the incised line, for example the three-dimensional models of the INSCRIBE project. The present drawings can confirm that a coded crescent is a crescent and that a coded bar is a bar. They cannot settle a disputed catalog number, and this study does not invent one.
