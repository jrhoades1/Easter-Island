## What "open" meant here

CC0 and CC BY, stated on the institution's page, were the licences that would have allowed a sign-by-sign look and, with credit, a copy in this repository. CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, a private-use line, and "all rights reserved" were recorded and left unused. Where the holder's page does not state a licence, the class is "not stated by the holder." A guess from the age of a negative was not used.

The drawings already checked in Round 5B stay under the CEIPP non-profit terms. They are not photographs, and they are not CC0 or CC BY. They were not fetched again.

## Why nothing was re-read

The passages that feed the headline counts are the Mamari calendar on C, line Gv6, the Staff bars on I, the Great Tradition on H, P, and Q, and the absence of sign `076` on R.

Berlin and Vienna are the holders that publish photographs with a licence line and a measured size. Berlin is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Vienna's object pages allow private use and send academic use to a request. Europeana records CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for the Vienna provider. Neither is CC0 or CC BY.

The Smithsonian page for R is the closest call. It prints CC0 on the viewer and, in the same block, says there are restrictions on re-using the media. The IIIF manifest points the licence at the Terms of Use page and marks CC0 under "Metadata Usage." The two frames of the board that the image service returned are 1280 pixels wide and about 350 pixels tall, with the lines of the inscription stacked in that height. Round 5B already found that a drawing line about 74 pixels tall can separate a crescent from a bar and cannot settle a catalog number. These frames are coarser. They were not used to look for sign `076`, and no code was changed.

The other headline objects have no holder-stated CC0 or CC BY photograph at all. The 6000×4000 picture associated with Small Santiago is a reproduction in Hanga Roa under the photographer's CC BY-SA 2.0, not a Museo Nacional file.
