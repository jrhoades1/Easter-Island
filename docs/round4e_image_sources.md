# Where better rongorongo images and tracings are

This is a survey of published and public routes. Nobody was contacted.

The practical split is: line drawings and reduced photographs are already online under a non-profit terms statement; metric 3D models exist for six objects and are viewable, with reuse restricted; most of the wood is in museums and church archives, and a person asks each holder. Keiti was destroyed in 1914. The Stephen-Chauvet fragment is in a private collection, not a museum.

## What an individual should ask for

1. Start with the CEIPP drawings and the six INSCRIBE models. That is the material a person can open today without a letter.
2. For a tablet that has no model, ask the holder for existing photographs and for permission to make new ones, and say the purpose (checking a transcription, not a commercial picture).
3. Ask whether a cast, rubbing, or RTI/3D file already exists before proposing a new scan. Berlin (O) and the staff (I) are the pieces where a new image would change the most, and O is the one most likely to be refused on conservation grounds.
4. Keep licenses separate. A drawing on the CEIPP site, a photograph in Fischer's book, a Smithsonian CC0 file, and an INSCRIBE mesh are four different permissions.
