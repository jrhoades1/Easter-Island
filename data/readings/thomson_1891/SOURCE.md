# Thomson 1891 — Ure Vaeiko

## Print source

William J. Thomson, "Te Pito te Henua, or Easter Island," in *Report of the U.S. National Museum under the direction of the Smithsonian Institution, for the year ending June 30, 1889* (Washington: Government Printing Office, 1891), pp. 447–552.

The visit was December 1886 (USS Mohican). The separate offprint is the same text. A scan is Internet Archive `tepitotehenuaor00thomgoog` (https://archive.org/details/tepitotehenuaor00thomgoog). That scan is public domain (U.S. government printing, 1891).

The OCR of that scan confirms the passages used here, including:

- Ure Vaeiko was not turning the photograph with the lines: "it became evident that he was not actually reading the characters. It was noticed that the shifting of position did not accord with the number of symbols on the lines, and afterwards when the photograph of another tablet was substituted, the same story was continued without the change being discovered." (Thomson 1891, p. 516.)
- The chant headed **ATUA MATARIRI** is tied to **Plates XXXVIII and XXXIX** (Thomson 1891, p. 520). The opening line in the scan is "Atua Matariri … Ki ai Kiroto, Kia Taporo, Kapu te Poporo."

Google's OCR of the scan breaks words at line ends, so the working text is the Kohaumotu page transcription, which says it follows the 1891 print:

- http://kohaumotu.org/rongorongo_org/thomson/514.html through `526.html`
- Snapshots: `pages/514.html` … `pages/526.html` (retrieved 2026-09-27)

Kohaumotu's notice (http://kohaumotu.org/rongorongo_org/copy.html): the site may be copied if the source is acknowledged and the use is non-profit. The underlying Thomson prose is public domain either way. These snapshots are not covered by this repository's MIT license.

## Which chant was printed under which plates

Thomson's own headings, not a later catalog:

| Chant in Thomson | Pages | Plates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apai | 517–518 (English follows) | XXXVI and XXXVII |
| Atua Matariri | 520–521 (English 521–522) | XXXVIII and XXXIX |
| Eaha to ran ariiki kete | 523–524 | XL and XLI |
| Father mourning / Ka ihi uiga | 525 | XLII and XLIII |
| Ate-a-renga-hokau iti poheraa | 526 | XLIV and XLV |

Salmon's English, printed by Thomson, is a translation of the chant. It is not a gloss of any glyph. Later readers (the Kohaumotu Rosetta notes) treat that English as unreliable. This track does not use it as a meaning.

Thomson p. 514 says the two tablets bought on the island were a large piece of driftwood and a smaller piece of toromiro. Thomson p. 515 says the photographs produced that night were of tablets owned by the bishop in Tahiti. The plate headings above are what he printed; they are not a statement that every plate was a bishop photograph.

The Santiago Staff (Barthel **I**) is not one of these plate headings. Steven R. Fischer, "Preliminary Evidence for Cosmogonic Texts in Rapanui's Rongorongo Inscriptions," *Journal of the Polynesian Society* 104 (1995), argued that the staff repeats the same kind of procreation verse as Atua Matariri, with glyph 76 standing in for the copula. That gloss is a **hypothesis**. It is not copied from Fischer 1997, and it is not treated as a reading. Jacques B. M. Guy rejected it (*Anthropos* 93, 1998; *Journal de la Société des Océanistes* 1998). Kohaumotu's summary is `rosetta/i.html`.
